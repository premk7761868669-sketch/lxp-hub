Sit-Ups and Crunches: Form and Progression

Crunches and sit-ups train the abdominal muscles that help you bend, twist and keep good posture. Exercise strengthens the muscle, but you cannot burn fat from one spot, so combine it with overall activity and good habits.

How to do it

  1. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat on the floor.
  2. Cross your arms on your chest or place fingertips lightly behind your head.
  3. Tighten your stomach and curl your shoulders up (crunch) or sit all the way up (sit-up).
  4. Lower back down slowly instead of dropping.

Common mistakes

  • Pulling on the neck with your hands.
  • Using momentum and jerking up.
  • Holding your breath. Breathe out as you come up.

How to progress

Start with controlled crunches, then slow the lowering phase to 3 seconds. Add reps gradually and move to full sit-ups when crunches feel easy. If you have back pain, ask a doctor and consider planks or dead bugs instead.
